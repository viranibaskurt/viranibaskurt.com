### Entry 2 — Step 1 "Configurable dot and background color" — DONE (1 try, plus one polarity discussion)
- Happened: Between sessions the user had already done unrelated prep work (converted the old shader `const`s to `uniform`s, built a live debug panel with sliders in webgl.js) — reconciled against the journal at reload, no conflict, not part of the syllabus. Also, unprompted, changed `field = 1.0 - smoothstep(...)` to `field = smoothstep(...)`, dropping the inversion.
- Off at first: The dropped inversion flips which end of the 0..1 range means "dot" vs "background" relative to the old grayscale code, so the rendered picture is the *exact color inverse* of the pre-Step-1 look (old: black dots on white bg: new default: white dots on black bg) — the written challenge outcome said "same picture as before," which turned out to be a wrong assumption on my (the tutor's) part, not a bug in the user's work. Walked the user through tracing the math by hand (raw field huge at a dot center -> smoothstep saturates to 1 -> old `1.0-1=0` = black there) to make them see the inversion explicitly, since they initially answered around the question rather than stating the old-vs-new comparison directly (took three exchanges to get a direct "0 is black, and that's opposite of what I see now").
- Clicked when: Once they traced through the arithmetic, they explained clearly that the inversion was intentional — they wanted white dots on black background as the actual target, and the "match old behavior" framing was never their goal, just my incorrect assumption when writing the challenge. Closed with a clean, correct verbal explanation: field = how much the current pixel is under a metaball's influence; 0 -> render background, 1 -> render dot color, which is exactly why `mix(background, dot, field)` produces the intended picture.
- Learning: Solid on `mix` as a 0..1 dial between two colors, and on reading what a variable's value *means* (not just its formula) by tracing one concrete pixel through the math by hand. No fix needed here since the "bug" was actually the tutor's wrong assumption, not their code — worth remembering for later steps: don't assume "same as before" is the goal without checking, especially once the user has started actively tuning appearance via the debug panel.
